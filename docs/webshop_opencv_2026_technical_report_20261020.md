# WEB&SHOP OpenCV AI Competition 2026 — technical report

Prepared 2026-10-20. WEB&SHOP is the submitted application. InventoryLens is its recognition, SKU registration, scanning, and inventory intelligence module. This report describes the working module demonstration and the separate evidence supporting future integration; it does not represent the entire recognition pipeline as live on AWS. See the [submission narrative and architecture diagram](webshop_opencv_2026_submission_20261020.md).

## Problem, users, and current application boundary

WEB&SHOP's primary business outcome is changing and tracking inventory after products are scanned and matched to its registry. The user chose an **add/subtract stock movement** model for the intended WEB&SHOP integration. InventoryLens supplies picture-based physical counts by SKU and location for that movement. A scan needs an explicit reason or linked business event to choose the direction; the pixels alone cannot tell receiving from sale, removal, or an audit. Single-photo counts can fail when an image is blurred or dark, when repeated products overlap, or when a detector misses an item or encloses several items in one box. WEB&SHOP aims to guide capture and make the proposed SKU/count list correctable before an authorized inventory change. Customers need a short capture and review flow; employees need repeatable shelf or inventory scans; business owners need SKU registration, reference-gallery control, and oversight of approved stock.

The existing local application in [`app.py`](../app.py) and [`owner_ui.py`](../owner_ui.py) supports owner registration and employee inventory workflows with a MobileSAM/ResNet18 recognition path. Its [`local_features.py`](../local_features.py) also uses OpenCV SIFT matching and RANSAC geometry to verify some ambiguous printed-label matches against registered reference images; this is a selective check, not a measured replacement for its embedding matcher. After a user reviews the proposed quantities and count scope, its existing approval action records the scan and **replaces** the selected SKU totals at the chosen location. This snapshot behavior differs from the requested add/subtract ledger and must not be presented as if movements are already implemented. The current record has timestamps and scan history; sales, returns, receiving, and continuous automatic sensing are not connected here. Its database and production inventory behavior were not changed for this competition work. The isolated [`live_demo.py`](../experiments/opencv5_guided_scan/live_demo.py) is a guided-scan module experiment that supports uploaded, camera, and frozen test photos and produces review-only JSON; it does not call that stock-update path. Customer photos are included in the test evidence; a customer-specific WEB&SHOP interface and shared production inference service are planned, not implemented here.

### Planned movement contract

A receiving scan would add its approved units; an authorized sale/removal scan would subtract its approved units. Each movement needs the registered SKU, location, signed quantity, action/reason, actor, time, and a scan or business-event identifier. The inventory service should store the movement and change the balance atomically, and repeated submission of the same scan must not apply it twice. A photo of five products already on a shelf is an **observation**, not automatically a `+5` movement; a stock audit would compare the observed count with the recorded balance and require approval of any adjustment. Multiple views of the same arrangement must represent one set of units. These are integration requirements, not functionality of the current AWS pilot or local snapshot app.

## Working OpenCV 5 capture component

The shared [`capture_gate.py`](../experiments/opencv5_guided_scan/capture_gate.py) runs in both the local replay and the AWS Lambda package. It decodes the input with OpenCV 5, records original dimensions, resizes to at most 1024 pixels on the long side, converts to grayscale, and measures Laplacian variance as a focus cue plus the percentages of dark and bright pixels. The provisional rule requests a retake for an unreadable or small image, excessive dark or bright pixels, or low focus variance; otherwise it proceeds to human product review. The measured runtime was OpenCV `5.0.0`; the pinned headless Lambda wheel was `5.0.0.93` and NumPy `2.5.4`.

The thresholds are illustrative and were selected with knowledge of the local examples. They are not calibrated for camera diversity, dark packaging, reflective labels, or independent quality labels. Every retake can be inspected and overridden by a person. The decision changes the next visible action: `retake` holds review until the operator captures a better photo or overrides, while `proceed` exposes review controls.

## Deployed AWS component and security

The existing WEB&SHOP pilot is in the user-confirmed selected Region `eu-north-1`. An operator explicitly clicks the AWS button in the local demo; the exact JPEG/PNG bytes are uploaded to a private S3 `incoming/` key. An S3 event invokes one on-demand Python 3.13, x86-64 Lambda with OpenCV 5. The function writes one private `results/` JSON with quality metrics, decision, OpenCV/NumPy versions, source key, and source SHA-256. The local demo rejects an AWS result whose image hash or decision disagrees with its selected photo/local check. Archived model predictions attach only to frozen photos with an exact source SHA-256 match.

The [resource manifest](../experiments/opencv5_guided_scan/aws_pilot/deployment_resources_20261020.json) names the bucket, Lambda, execution role, log group, and release ZIP. S3 Block Public Access, SSE-S3, TLS-only access, a narrow role, an `incoming/` notification filter, a one-day object lifecycle, and three-day log retention are configured. The Lambda is limited to 512 MB and a 15-second timeout. The project did not allow the proposed reserved-concurrency setting of two, so it is not active; the batch uploader sent one image and waited for its result before the next. There is no API Gateway, public endpoint, GPU, ECR repository, or cross-Region resource in this pilot. The existing pilot is retained through submission at the user's request.

The user-confirmed AWS credit balance is **$100** on the Free plan. That balance is separate from the **$10 maximum new pilot spend** the user authorized. The [measured pilot result](opencv5_aws_capture_pilot_result_20261020.md) estimates the audited batch's Lambda portion at about $0.00117 before credits; storage, requests, logs, taxes, and any other project usage add to that, and posted billing can lag. The one-day lifecycle and 200-current-input demo guard help bound routine use, but neither is an automated cumulative dollar cap. Regional rate sources are linked in the pilot result.

## Evaluation and reproducibility

The frozen AWS upload manifest has **193 source paths and 176 distinct SHA-256 images** across early K18/WOW/coconut tests, grocery tests, and the later owner, employee, and customer test groups. The later group contributes 42 Owner_test, 25 employee_test, and five customer_test photos to the source-path inventory. All 176 unique originals produced matching AWS image hashes, capture actions, and metrics against the local baseline: 152 `proceed`, 24 `retake`, zero mismatches. Two in-memory blurred/dark controls returned the intended retake prompts. One later E01 demo run returned a matching hash, OpenCV 5.0.0, and `proceed`. These are capture-gate agreement and branching results, not image-quality accuracy or end-to-end SKU accuracy.

The [local audit summary](../experiments/opencv5_guided_scan/runs/aws_pilot_20261020/audit_summary_20261020.json), [local expected decisions](../experiments/opencv5_guided_scan/aws_pilot/local_expected_decisions_20261020.csv), [upload manifest](../experiments/opencv5_guided_scan/aws_pilot/photo_upload_manifest_20261020.csv), [downloaded results](../experiments/opencv5_guided_scan/runs/aws_pilot_20261020/results), and [separate live-demo result](../experiments/opencv5_guided_scan/runs/aws_pilot_20261020/live_demo_smoke_result_20261020.json) make the claims auditable after S3's short retention expires. The batch audit recorded 179 Lambda REPORT entries including its import test, 136,546 ms total billed duration, 4,988 ms maximum billed duration, and 240 MB maximum reported memory. The later demo run is outside that frozen batch audit.

The existing [new-product registration and scene-review experiment](../experiments/new_product_scanning_20261020/README.md) keeps the 72 owner/employee/customer photos, candidate SKU review, and annotation outputs separate from production. Its [dataset audit](../experiments/new_product_scanning_20261020/audit/dataset_audit.md) records photo provenance; the [GPU benchmark decision](../data/recognition_checks/final_comparative_gpu_benchmark_20261018/benchmark_audit_architecture_decision_20261019.md) and [complete audit](../data/recognition_checks/final_comparative_gpu_benchmark_20261018/complete_final_gpu_benchmark_audit_20261019.md) retain the earlier model evidence. These folders remain in place as supporting technical evidence for WEB&SHOP.

The newest [local recognition review](../experiments/new_product_scanning_20261020/results/latest_recognition_review_20261020.html) makes the ten provisional SKU groups and 34 approved MobileSAM reference masks visible. A paired, leave-one-owner-photo-out SigLIP2 retrieval run found 24/34 correct top-1 choices with box crops and 25/34 with masked crops; masking corrected one oatmeal-versus-rice-mash choice and did not change the correctness of the other 33. Both query and gallery crops used the selected representation, so this controlled owner-reference comparison does not isolate gallery-only masking, measure scene accuracy, or prove general mask benefit. A new [v3 30-scene input bundle](../experiments/new_product_scanning_20261020/runpod_inference/README_RUNPOD.md) packages the same 34 references as both unmasked and masked crops, with a runner that reuses each DINO box and query embedding across galleries. No new-scene GPU inference has run or been imported. Reference masking cannot repair a DINO detector miss or merged box; the matched gallery-only comparison awaits GPU execution and verified scene labels.

An additional [local OpenCV 5 recognition feasibility check](../experiments/new_product_scanning_20261020/results/opencv5_recognition_feasibility_20261020.md) decoded retail barcodes in 3/42 owner photos. A simple OpenCV ORB feature matcher got 8/34 top-1 on the paired owner-photo cohort with either box or masked crops, below SigLIP2's 24/34 and 25/34 on that cohort. These are exploratory reference tests, not scene results. The planned value remains **counting products from photos against a confirmed registry**. OpenCV item-specific capture guidance is a candidate addition to that path; barcode decoding is optional SKU verification for an already counted item. Neither is deployed in the AWS pilot or connected to production inventory.

## Human control, limitations, and next integration

The reviewer can accept or correct a SKU, mark an overlap, split a multi-product box into manual items, remove a false proposal, and add a missed product. E01 has a user-confirmed eight-item total but an assistant-interpreted per-SKU split; B07 needs the user-confirmed Fentimans in addition to other reviewed items; B10 is one user-confirmed ten-item arrangement across two views and must not be summed twice. These corrections are in [separate notes](../experiments/opencv5_guided_scan/runs/local_replay_20261020/review_corrections_20261020.md); the original benchmark files and browser export remain unchanged. No demonstration output is committed to inventory.

Before full WEB&SHOP integration, audit exact product variants and reference-gallery rights/coverage, label separate physical instances and genuine unknown products, evaluate DINO and alternatives under matched settings, score SigLIP2 correct/wrong/unknown on reviewed single-product crops, measure correction time, and choose a supported runtime for live models. A production inventory update would require an authorized role-specific confirmation path and separate approval. Only approved test images should be shown to judges; review rights for third-party models, photos, packaging, and any submitted media before publication. The [competition rules](https://opencv26.devpost.com/) require accurate capability claims and rights to submitted material.
